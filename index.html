AI in My Corner
2026-11-14

This is the first post I've written for this blog in five years, thanks to AI.

That's a weird sentence to write. To be clear, the words in this blog post are not written by AI. I've been using and thinking about AI and all its broad impacts for a while now. A lot has changed in the past couple of years but my writing remains my own.

I started this blog a dozen years ago during a flurry of personal creativity. Within the span of about a year I also launched nuclides.org and eclipsetracks.org. The venerable familygiftregistry.net, already in its ninth year back then and serving a bit over a thousand users at that time, continued its steady pace of iterative development.

Over time available energy to keep old projects fresh waned, as these things tend to do. I love long term projects but these were becoming long term projects, and going it solo is tough. The internet evolves around them like water changing for a fish. Obsolescence and vulnerability creep in at a steadily accelerating pace. Time makes bit rot and time is easier to accrue with each passing day. It piled up while life's other demands on available time ramped up too. It all comes from the same stuff. We're all riding the unpredictable history-spanning wave that is human technology and civilization.

Then a couple of years ago or so what we would call AI today started popping up in the workplace. I've got a lot of big thoughts (mostly questions) about how this AI thing will continue to evolve. But this post isn't about mass unemployment, deepfakes, existential threats, etc. This is about the small stuff. My little corner of the internet that I would keep up for the long haul. Not the first little corner I made by any stretch, but the first that needed to last. And after 21 years my corner had begun to wither a bit. Everything still worked of course, but there were cracks all over the foundation.

This is the aside to note that literally all software everywhere has cracks in the foundation so I let's not be too melodramatic...

At any rate, generative AI coding steadily and quickly became a new standard coding practice in my professional world. As a programmer with over a quarter of a century of experience one of the tightest bottlenecks I deal with is implementation. I have no shortage of ideas and often I can quickly reason about the specifics of a pretty good solution to tackle all sorts of problems. That's the fun of it, after all, solving puzzles. The syntax, though... that's what eats the time. Because it's computers. Everything is exact and unavoidably complex. Sometimes implementation is fun (that is, the typing of every line) but especially when there's just so much it's usually a grind.

But the AI doesn't care. It's great at the grind. It's good to bounce ideas with and poke holes but when I'm opinionated and confident in my awareness of my project I can make it realize my vision so much faster than I could type it out myself. And boy does that ever drive some inspiration.

So I finally found the time and energy to bring generative AI to my personal projects. My little corner of the internet, now old enough to drink in the United States and with a decent patina, is now ready to shine again.

In just a few weeks' time:

  • FamilyGiftRegistry.net · major critical dependency updates, maxed out email scores, security fixes, and expanded admin tooling
  • Nuclides.org · major dependency updates, new content, and interactivity improvements
  • EclipseTracks.org · major dependency updates, performance upgrades, and accurate umbral shadows
  • Frencil.com · restoration of broken external link content and added a dark mode toggle for fun (and for my eyeballs)

This really clears a massive chunk of the tech debt plate across a lot of web space (for one person). I've got plenty of modernization left to do in places but I've found myself very quickly back in a position where I can dream up new features again. I can dive into issues I filed on my own repos years ago with fresh eyes and new capabilities. It's pretty inspiring.

So AI helped breathe new life into my little corner of the internet. That definitely affects more people than just me as (other than this blog) these projects are used and enjoyed by people I'll never meet. Family Gift Registry especially... that's used by thousands of people quite regularly so I definitely have to keep that thing going.

To me the best parts of the internet have always been smaller places that are wholesome and unique. I like to think the projects I run are such places. At the macro scale AI is accelerating and destabilizing lots of stuff. I continue to watch with scrutiny, knowing we're all powerless to get off this ride, wherever it's going. So, for the duration, I've got my corner of the internet where AI has been helping out lately. And things have never been better.

--

Christoph Clark
